# VMA 20-Minute Pilot Fit Call

Updated 17 September 2026. Use this script for the current performance pilot. It replaces the old monthly/annual offer for new pilot conversations. Existing clients keep their accepted agreements.

**Purpose:** Find out whether VMA can help this business acquire and handle profitable new work. Finish with one decision: prepare a written pilot scope, resolve one specific gap, or decline the pilot for now.

## 0–2 minutes: Find the outcome

“Thanks for making time. What type of job would you like more of, and how many more could your team realistically handle?”

“I’ll ask how clients find you, what happens when they inquire and whether the numbers make sense. Then we can decide whether the pilot fits.”

Record the main service, service area, available capacity and person responsible for new inquiries.

## 2–7 minutes: Find the bottleneck

Ask these as a conversation, following the owner's answers:

- “Where do your best new clients come from today?”
- “When an inquiry comes in while you’re working, what happens next?”
- “Who responds, gives the estimate and follows up?”
- “What usually stops an interested client from booking: timing, price, trust, service fit or something else?”
- “Do your clients use WhatsApp? Would starting there make it easier for them to reach you?”
- “Do you already have a Facebook business page, an advertising account and a Google Business Profile?”

Lack of a Meta account is a setup consideration, not an automatic rejection. Explain that VMA guides the required setup; account access and approval affect launch readiness.

Reflect the answer back:

“From what you’ve told me, the main gap is [specific gap]. The first thing we should test is [specific change], measured by [relevant result].”

## 7–10 minutes: Check that more work can pay

“For the service we would advertise, roughly how much do you collect per job, and how much is left after the labor and materials needed to deliver it?”

“Can we connect each inquiry to its quote, job outcome and actual payment?”

Use the owner's figures. Revenue is not profit. The 20% VMA fee, Meta spend and any agreed additional costs must leave enough margin to make the work worthwhile.

**Internal arithmetic example, not a forecast:** If all $1,000 of a job's collected revenue qualifies, and $600 goes to direct delivery costs, $400 remains before acquisition costs. A $200 VMA fee leaves $200 before that job's share of Meta spend, any additional agreed costs and overhead. Do not present the fee example as a guaranteed return.

## 10–13 minutes: Explain VMA through their gap

“VMA creates and manages ads that let people message your business on WhatsApp, then sets up intake and a handover so your team can pick up the conversation with useful details. We also manage your Google Business Profile and review process so local clients can understand your services and assess your business.”

“The aim is to help more suitable inquiries become paid work while taking the agreed marketing tasks off your plate. Your team still decides the price, wins the job and delivers the service.”

Choose the explanation that matches the bottleneck:

| Their gap | Explain the relevant work |
|---|---|
| Not enough suitable inquiries | Campaign setup, creative and targeting around an agreed service and area; accurate Google profile information. Test inquiry quality rather than promising a lead volume. |
| Clients cannot confidently assess the business | Clear services, useful photos, honest review requests and professional replies. No promised rating or ranking. |
| Inquiries stall while the team is busy | Configured WhatsApp intake, permitted follow-up, calendar connection where agreed, and a clear handover to the person who can quote and close. |
| No time to manage marketing | VMA handles the agreed setup and management. The owner supplies accurate information, approvals, a response contact and job/payment records. |

Google improvements support ongoing visibility; a 30-day advertising pilot does not prove every longer-term Google or reputation outcome.

## 13–16 minutes: Explain the pilot and the choice afterward

Read these two paragraphs as written. They match the pricing page:

“Start with 30 live campaign days and no setup fee. Your $450 advertising budget goes directly to Meta. VMA’s performance fee is 20% of qualifying collected revenue under the agreed pilot terms. If no qualifying revenue is collected, there is no VMA performance fee. Meta advertising costs still apply.”

“Any applicable platform, number or usage costs are agreed before launch. After the pilot, you decide whether to continue with a separately agreed scope, price and terms.”

Then connect the written scope to their business:

“Before launch, we agree what revenue qualifies, how we track it, the costs, who handles inquiries and what happens when the pilot ends. Which part would you like me to explain?”

Do not substitute “risk-free,” “only $450 at risk,” “nothing owed to anyone,” “cancel anytime” or “you keep every automation.” Those statements are broader than the current offer. Early termination, later collections and handover follow the signed pilot agreement.

## 16–20 minutes: Make one decision

1. **Capacity and a workable service margin?** If no, address that before buying more traffic.
2. **A named person can quote, follow up and fulfill the work?** If no, establish that responsibility first.
3. **WhatsApp fits the intended clients and the required accounts can be set up?** If uncertain, resolve the channel/access issue before launch.
4. **The business can fund the $450 Meta budget and any agreed costs without relying on promised results, and maintain source/job/payment records?** If no, do not push the pilot.
5. **A specific gap, test and measures are agreed?** If yes, prepare the written scope. Continuing after the pilot is a separate decision.

### If there is a fit

“Based on [their gap and capacity], I suggest a pilot focused on [service and area]. We would track suitable inquiries, human follow-up, booked work, qualifying collected revenue and the costs. Shall we prepare the written scope for you to review?”

Next steps: written scope and terms → setup form → guided access/setup call → campaign approval → confirmed live start date. Booking this call does not start services or authorize a charge.

### If one gap must be resolved

“Before spending on ads, we need to resolve [specific gap]. Let’s make that the next step, then revisit whether the pilot makes sense.”

### If they are not ready

“The pilot doesn’t look like the right next step yet. If you want a starting point, the free online presence audit can help you see what needs attention.”

## After the call: Record only what moves the decision

- Priority service and area; realistic available capacity.
- Main bottleneck and the first test.
- Job value, estimated direct delivery cost and fee-base questions to resolve.
- Named inquiry owner, WhatsApp suitability and account readiness.
- Agreed measures, costs to confirm and records available.
- Decision, next action, responsible person and date.
