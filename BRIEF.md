## 1. BUSINESS SUMMARY

**What VMA Management is**
VMA Management LLC is a Florida company that builds and runs the front end of a local service business's customer flow: a modern website that gets the business found, plus a system that answers every call, text, and form the second it comes in — even when the owner is on a roof, under a truck, or on another job. One flat monthly fee, nothing upfront, cancel any time. The owner keeps working; VMA makes sure the call turns into a booked job instead of a voicemail nobody hears.

**Niche / ICP**
Owner-operated home services and auto businesses in Broward County (Coral Springs, Plantation, Davie, Weston, Cooper City, Parkland, Coconut Creek, Pembroke Pines, Fort Lauderdale, Pompano). Secondary: Miami-Dade. English-speaking owner. 1–15 employees. Trades: HVAC, roofing, plumbing, electrical, garage doors, pest, pressure washing, auto repair, auto detailing, mobile mechanics.

**Qualifiers:** has employees or more than one truck; answers texts; permitted and licensed; either no website, a dead website, or fewer than ~15 Google reviews.

**The problem we solve**
These owners work with their hands. When the phone rings they're holding a tool. The call goes to voicemail, the customer calls the next name on Google, and the job is gone in under five minutes. The owner never finds out. Separately, many are close to invisible online — no site, an abandoned site, or a thin Google profile with old reviews.

**Primary business outcome**
**More of the calls they already get turn into booked jobs.** Not "marketing." Not "AI." One outcome.

## 2. OFFER SUMMARY

**Commercial terms:** one flat monthly fee · no setup fee · month-to-month, cancel any time · live in 30 days or full refund. **The dollar figure does not appear on the website.** It is delivered in the pre-call video and confirmed on the call.

**What's included**

**Positioning**

**Do NOT emphasize:** any platform or software name (never) · "AI," "automation," "CRM," "funnel" · feature lists longer than five · credentials VMA doesn't have · WhatsApp (built, silent unless raised) · anything implying an existing client roster.

## 6. TRUST AND PROOF STRATEGY

Zero clients, zero testimonials. Trade proof for **specificity, accountability, and risk reversal**. Never manufacture social proof — one fake logo or invented testimonial, discovered, ends the business.

**Signals available right now — use all of them**
- **Legal reality:** VMA Management LLC, a registered Florida company, with a real address, a real phone, and a real email printed in the open. No date attached — the entity's age is not the trust signal, its reachability is.
- **Full NAP** (name, address, phone) visible in the footer and the accountability block. Most competitors at this level show only a form.
- **A named human** who answers, in first person, signing the section.
- **The guarantee:** live in 30 days or full refund. Honest, fully inside your control, and it converts.
- **Terms stated three times** on the page: hero microcopy, terms section, FAQ.
- **The "not for" list.** Publicly turning business away is the cheapest credibility signal a new business has.
- **The live demo offer:** "On the call I'll text your own phone from your own business number so you can watch it happen." Demonstration beats testimony.
- **The site itself** is the work sample.
- **Real privacy and terms pages.** Almost no competitor at this level has them; their presence reads as legitimacy.

**Handling limited proof honestly**
- Never write "trusted by," "hundreds of businesses," "our clients," "we've helped."
- Say the true thing where it's an advantage: *"I keep a small number of clients so each one gets real attention."* True today, correct at a capacity of three.
- Ship **no** testimonial section. Build it styled and HTML-commented-out, ready to switch on the day a real client says yes.
- The first testimonial goes live with a real name, real business, real city, or not at all.

**Add later, in order:** (1) founder photo; (2) first client's before/after review count and site screenshot; (3) two named testimonials; (4) one case study with a real recovered-job number.

## 7. DESIGN DIRECTION

**Style: "Field-grade, not startup."** High-contrast, structural, built to be read in daylight on a cracked phone screen. Confident and plain — the visual equivalent of a clean, lettered work van. Deliberately not soft, not pastel, not SaaS.

**Colors — sampled directly from the supplied logo files. These are the brand. No other colors.**

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--navy` | `#082963` | Header, footer, headlines, body text, dark section bands |
| `--cyan` | `#00D7FF` | The single accent. CTA fills, the reply bubble, one hairline detail |
| `--white` | `#FFFFFF` | Content background |
| `--paper` | `#F1F4F9` | Alternating section background (cool, tinted toward navy) |
| `--rule` | `#D7DEE8` | Hairlines and card borders |
| `--muted` | `#55637A` | Sub-labels, fine print, footer secondary text |

**Contrast rules — verified, follow exactly**
- Navy text on cyan = 8.1:1. **All cyan buttons use navy text.** ✅
- White on navy = 13.9:1 ✅ · Cyan on navy = 8.1:1 ✅
- **Cyan on white = 1.7:1. FAILS.** Never use cyan for text, links, or thin strokes on a light background. Cyan is a *fill* color only.
- No gradients. No colored shadows. No third accent.

**Typography**
- **Display: Archivo 700/800**, tight tracking, large. Headlines read stamped, not styled. A sans headline is more legible for this audience than a serif.
- **Body: Inter 400/600**, 17–18px mobile, generous line height.
- **Utility: JetBrains Mono 400**, small, timestamps inside the phone demo only.
- The serif in the logo is the **wordmark only** and never appears as page type. That contrast is intentional: it makes the lockup read as a mark rather than as text.
- Self-host as woff2, latin subset, `font-display: swap`. No font CDN.

**Logo usage — the assets you have, and how they map**

| File | What it is | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| `VMA logo.png` (2229×975, navy background) | Full lockup, mark + wordmark | Sticky header, left. **This is why the header is navy** — the baked-in navy background disappears against a navy bar, so no re-export is needed. |
| `VMAlogo.png` (1025×975, navy square) | Mark only, square | Favicon set, apple-touch-icon, footer mark |
| `VMA Transparent.png` (cyan mark + white wordmark, transparent) | Lockup for dark backgrounds | Footer, and the source for the OG image |

Export or request an **SVG** of the mark before launch — you built this in Canva, so export it as SVG there. PNG logos in a header are either blurry on retina or heavy. Ship with a 2× compressed PNG if the SVG isn't ready; swap it later.

**Layout**
Single column. Text max-width ~680px, hero wider. Left-aligned throughout — no centered paragraphs. Section breaks are a 1px `--rule` line or a navy band, never a floating card with a shadow. Buttons: 4px radius, minimum 56px tall, full-width on mobile, cyan fill with navy label.

**Signature element (the one thing the page is remembered by)**
The hero phone screen — a hand-built HTML/CSS message thread, no image file. A muted grey row reading `Missed call · 2:14 PM`, then a sent bubble in `--cyan` with navy text: *"Hi — this is Mike at Coral Springs AC. Sorry we missed you, we're on a job. What do you need help with?"* and a mono timestamp: `2:14 PM · 8 seconds later`. Static on mobile; on desktop the bubble fades in once on scroll. That is the entire product in one image, and it's the same thing demonstrated live on the call. Spend the design budget here; keep everything else quiet.

**What to avoid so it doesn't read as AI-generated**
- Cream `#F4F1EA` backgrounds with serif display type and terracotta accents — the current default look of AI-built sites, instantly recognizable.
- Purple-to-blue gradient heroes, glassmorphism, blurred color blobs, dark mode with a single acid accent.
- Three-column icon-card grids: icon, bold noun, 30 words.
- `01 / 02 / 03` numbered markers on anything that isn't a real sequence. Only "How it works" qualifies.
- Fake dashboards, upward-trending charts, logo bars, testimonials.
- Stock photography of any kind.
- Scroll animation on every section. **One** animation exists on this site: the reply bubble.
- Pill buttons with soft drop shadows.

## 10. THE LIVE SHOWCASE — BUILD ORDER AND SCOPE FENCE

**The goal, stated correctly:** a prospect should be able to touch VMA's own system and feel the product before the call. That instinct is right and it's the strongest proof available to a founder with no testimonials. It is also the single most effective way to spend three weeks building instead of selling. This section exists to fence it.

### The distinction you have to hold

You are conflating two different assets. They prove different things and only one of them can live on VMA's own account.

| | **Asset A — VMA's live system** | **Asset B — the demo sub-account** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The website, chat widget, and (786) 926-8584 wired to instant text-back and a shared inbox | A fictional contractor build, e.g. "Coral Springs AC" |
| What it proves | *This guy responds instantly. The thing he sells actually works.* | *This is what MY business would look like running it* |
| Why you need both | A contractor doesn't want to see a marketing company's system. He wants to see a contractor's system. | Asset A proves responsiveness; only Asset B proves trade fit |
| Status | Build this week | Already required by the sales SOP — build it once, use it on every call |

### Build order, ranked by proof-per-hour

1. **Website live** with (786) 926-8584 in the header. *Today.*
2. **Missed-call text-back on 786-926-8584.** ~30 minutes. This is the demo. The moment a prospect calls that number and it goes unanswered, they receive the exact product you're selling. Nothing else on this list comes close on proof-per-hour.
3. **Chat widget on the site**, routing to the same inbox as calls and texts. ~30 minutes. Second-highest value: a prospect who types a question at 9pm and gets an instant, useful reply has experienced the offer.
4. **A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration** in Trust Center. Start it the day the site is live — the privacy page is a prerequisite and the approval queue is not under your control. Unregistered automated SMS gets filtered by carriers.
5. **Demo sub-account (Asset B)** built out as a fictional Broward contractor, for screen-share on calls.
6. **AI voice answering — after-hours only, and last.** See the risks below.

### Explicitly out of scope — do not build these for VMA

- **A Google Business Profile for VMA Management.** Three reasons, any one of which is sufficient: a Brickell suite address is very likely a virtual or coworking office, which is not eligible for verification and risks suspension; a marketing company's profile proves nothing to a contractor; and you have no reviews on it, so it demonstrates the opposite of what you're selling. Kill it.
- **Review automation running on VMA's own profile.** You have no customers to request reviews from. The only way to populate it is to manufacture reviews, which is both a Google ban and an FTC violation. Demonstrate the review mechanism inside Asset B instead.

### Voice AI — the risks nobody flags until they've been billed

- **Cost exposure.** A public phone number on a live website attracts spam and robocalls. If an AI voice agent answers every one of them and you're billed per minute, you have built a machine that converts junk traffic into invoices. Set a hard usage cap before you enable it.
- **It can disprove your own product.** A clumsy AI answering the line during business hours, in front of a prospect who is calling to buy, is a worse outcome than voicemail. Answer live during business hours. Let the AI take after-hours only.
- **Florida is a two-party consent state.** Any recording or transcription needs a disclosure at the top of the call. Build the disclosure into the greeting, not the terms page.
- **Inbound only, permanently.** Outbound AI voice in Florida's litigation environment is a category of risk with no upside at your stage.
